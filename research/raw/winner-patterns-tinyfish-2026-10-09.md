## Repeated signals

- Submit new work and make the event-period history obvious; remove doubts about originality.
- Start with the problem, scope to one thing, and make the demo the pitch.
- A simple workflow that works beats a large feature list.
- Judges notice polished UI, clear storytelling, and a finished product that they can understand quickly.
- Meet every basic requirement before adding novelty; incomplete submissions lose before technical quality is considered.
- Strong winners show a real workflow, not only a dashboard or a claim. GitLab's LORE winner was described as a product, shipped with extensive tests; other winners opened reviews, used real deployments, or showed decisions with evidence.
- “Human approval,” deterministic gates, honest limitations and a visible fallback reduce risk in agentic or policy-heavy demos.

## Implication for DustQueue

DustQueue starts with one named inspector, shows one ranked queue and one closure, uses a polished but small UI, and makes the AWS path visible. The score is deterministic; the fixture data is labelled; an inspector approves the final action. This directly matches the pattern above and the Environmental Hacks rubric.
